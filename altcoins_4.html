47 Layer 1 blockchains, 7 portfolio slots

Layer 1s are the base networks everything else runs on. Owning the token is like owning a share of a digital country: it wins when people build, pay and park money on that chain. Layer 1 is the biggest category in our universe, so it gets the most slots, split evenly between large caps and small caps.

The six criteria and their weights

Same six-part framework as Videos 2 and 4, adapted for blockchains: for a chain, "revenue" is the fees users pay on it and its apps, and ETFs and Wall Street partners weigh more because they decide where institutional money flows. Every chain is scored 1–5 per criterion; the weighted total is out of 5.

Before we rank · The whole category

Wall Street is now choosing chains: ETFs and tokenisation decide the winners

In 2026 the SEC's generic listing rules opened the door to altcoin ETFs, and banks started settling real money on-chain: DTCC Treasuries on Canton, The Clearing House on Quant, BlackRock and Franklin funds on Avalanche and Aptos. Most Layer 1s are still 80–99% below their highs, but the gap between chains that are being used and chains that are only being traded is getting wider.

1 · Usage that reaches the tokenFees paid on the chain and its apps, and whether they are burned or paid to stakers. A busy chain with tiny fees and no burn gives holders little.
2 · Price it against activityMarket cap compared with the money on the chain (TVL and stablecoins). A $10B chain with $70M of deposits is priced on hope.
3 · Institutions and unlocksA live spot ETF and Wall Street users bring new buyers; big unlocks bring new sellers. Both are on the calendar.
